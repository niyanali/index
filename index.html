About

THE PHILOSOPHY

My process is lean by necessity, powerful by design. I shoot, direct, light, edit, color, and sound-design my own work. Not because I have to, but because I believe the soul of a film lives in its consistency of vision. One mind, one lens, one uncompromising standard.

I started making films with a stolen phone and a broken tripod back in 2016. No film school. No industry connections. No safety net. What I had wasn’t a roadmap. It was a fever an obsession with the way light hits a room and a stubborn, borderline violent refusal to make anything mediocre. While others were waiting for permission or the perfect budget, I was out in the dirt, learning how to make a cheap lens bleed emotion.

Halfway through my third year of university as a Computer Science student, I looked at the code on my screen and realized I was staring down the barrel of a lifetime I didn't want. A safe path, a predictable 9-to-5 desk job, a quiet corporate routine. I didn't want a secure office. I wanted to build worlds. So, I walked out. I dropped out of university not because it was hard, but because the hunger to do what I love was too loud to ignore. I traded steady algorithms for unpredictable art, risking everything to build a life dictated by my own eyes, not a corporate spreadsheet.

Eight years later, the scale has changed, but the hunger hasn't. I’ve directed campaigns for major brands that once felt entirely unreachable, screened work at festivals I used to watch from the cheap seats, and built a body of work that speaks louder than any institutional credential ever could. I went from surviving the frame to completely commanding it.

My process remains lean by necessity, powerful by design. I don’t just show up to direct—I shoot, light, edit, color, and sound-design my own work. Not out of ego, and not because I have to. I do it because I believe the soul of a film lives entirely in its consistency of vision. If the friction of the world doesn't make it onto the screen, it’s just content. But when you collapse the distance between the spark of an idea and the final master render, you get something else entirely.

One mind. One lens. One uncompromising standard. This isn’t a career—it’s an obsession.
